An on-device watcher for any RSS/Atom feed or public JSON API you choose: it sends a local notification when a new entry appears or a keyword matches. Everything runs on the device with unlimited monitors, scheduled checks and change-diffing.

Pupikes Site Monitor watches any website for you, with four tools. 1) Search now: type a site and a phrase and instantly see whether the text is present right now, like a quick search, with no alarm. 2) Daily text watch: add a site and a phrase and the app checks once a day whether that text appears; when it does, you get a notification and your chosen alarm music. 3) Daily image match: pick a reference image, and the app checks once a day whether an identical image (matched first by exact byte size and then byte for byte) appears on the site; on an exact match it alarms. 4) Daily status watch: add a site and be alerted the moment it changes between online and offline. You choose the music that plays on any alarm. Everything runs on the device with unlimited watches; no account, no cloud, no tracking. Also includes the original RSS/JSON feed monitoring with keyword matching and scheduled checks. 15-language interface, no ads.

New Tools screen with six sections: 1) Uptime — online/offline history and response time with a chart; 2) SSL — when the domain certificate expires and days left; 3) Price — track a price on any page with history and change; 4) Changes — which text was added or removed since your last visit; 5) Board — overall status of everything you watch and a weekly report to share; 6) Transfer — export/import of all monitors and ready templates (Telegram, YouTube, Reddit, GitHub). Checks run once per app launch to save traffic.

Overview screen on start: all tools on one board — uptime, SSL certificates, prices, page changes, RSS/JSON monitors, daily watches, board with weekly report, transfer and the feed catalog — each card with live lines from your data, plus a search box across all your news. A fresh install comes with 5 sample sites (wikipedia.org, bbc.com, github.com, weather.com, example.org) and 14 days of generated history, clearly marked as samples, so you can see every feature at work right away; one tap removes them.
